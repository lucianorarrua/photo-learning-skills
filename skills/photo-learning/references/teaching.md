# Demanding, adaptive teaching

Assess two things separately: recognition of the visible effect and the learner's
own explanation of the trade-off relative to intent. Evidence is provisional and
specific to this experiment; a self-description such as “experienced” is not a
demonstration. Record the prompting that made an answer possible.

## Respond to evidence

| Observed response | Tutor action | Evidence to preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Looks better,” agreement, or repetition of your words | Ask which region changed, in what way, and what became less suitable elsewhere; request a concrete comparison | Initial answer insufficient; subsequent recognition/explanation and prompt used |
| Correct effect and a reasoned trade-off in the learner's words | Name the specific achievement; increase challenge with a transfer prediction, competing priority, or reduced prompting | What was demonstrated and what transfer remains untested |
| Uncertainty, wrong prediction, frustration | Offer a concrete region to compare or a contrast between two possible effects; reduce to one small control change and give a direct explanation if needed | Hint/explanation supplied; recognition after assistance remains assisted |
| Learner wants to stop/change topics | Accept immediately, preserve partial evidence and unresolved point, offer a brief takeaway | Interrupted/pending outcome, not a passing assessment |

Ask one focused question at a time. Allow thinking time; avoid turning closure
into an interrogation. If a learner cannot yet explain after scaffolding, leave
that understanding unproven and suggest a smaller future exercise.

## Ground critique

- **Technical fact:** attribute reported settings/metadata/measurements to the
  actual tool and state. Exposure setting changes do not themselves prove clipping,
  sensor recovery, noise, or a particular rendered effect.
- **Visual observation:** describe only pixels actually inspected, naming regions
  and the preview state. A dark region is not proof of lost RAW data; a soft preview
  is not a reliable focus diagnosis. State uncertainty and seek supported evidence
  for histogram, clipping, decoder/fallback status, or color-fidelity claims.
- **Aesthetic suggestion:** tie alternatives to the learner's declared intention
  and phrase them as choices. A deliberate silhouette can coherently sacrifice
  subject detail; ask whether lost separation or background emphasis is intended
  rather than automatically lifting shadows. Challenge consequences that conflict
  with the learner's own intent, even when the adjustment is deliberate.

For example, if the intent is a silhouette, ask whether the outline still separates
from the background. If the intent is a detailed portrait, ask how the dark face
serves that intent. Judge the reasoning and observed consequence, not compliance
with a preferred brightness or a prescribed slider value.
